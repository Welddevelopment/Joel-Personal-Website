The story

I'm Joel. I'm 15, and I build things and do growth for startups.

While most people my age were playing Roblox, I was staring at it seeing a massive gap: thousands of developers and studios building on it, and no real way for them to find each other. So I decided to build one — a matching platform called weld. I'd never written a line of code. I taught myself, because the thing needed to exist — and shipped the landing page the same day I started coding. That was the first code I ever wrote.

weld. never found a business model, so I killed it. But it left me with the one skill that's mattered ever since: I can build the thing I imagine.

Barely a month after I figured out what a startup even was, I was doing Growth at a YC-backed company. I didn't apply through a form. I knew exactly how a YC founder would read a message from a 15-year-old: someone desperate to put "growth intern" on a CV. So I gave them nothing to dismiss — I spent my holiday break sending YC founders unsolicited growth audits, 10+ hours a day, one company per day: specific teardowns of exactly what I'd fix. Until one of them said yes.

Now I lead GTM there. On the side I keep showing up to hackathons — the first one only a few weeks into coding, where I was the youngest in the room and barely survived. A couple of 2nd-place finishes since. Ship fast, cut what doesn't work, start the next thing.

What I'm doing now

Leading GTM at Tsenta (YC S26), an AI job-application tool. I build the things I strategise — like a "doom timer" that tells job seekers how long their search will take and quietly funnels them into the product.

On the side: an 11+ tutoring agency I co-founded, and an open-source developer tool I'm building in the open.

Where this goes

Building isn't a side thing — it's the thing. I put in 8 hours a day on schooldays, after school and around it.

The plan: finish GCSEs, drop out, and go to YC as a founder. Not a backup plan — the plan.
